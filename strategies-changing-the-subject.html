Taking Turns

Knowing when and how to change the subject is one of the trickier parts of conversation for many Aspies. We can stay on a favourite topic long after the other person has lost interest, or switch so abruptly that it confuses them. The good news is that changing the subject is a skill with a simple, learnable pattern.

First, learn to spot when it is time to change. Neurotypical people rarely say "I am bored" — instead they give quieter signals. Watch for them beginning to avoid eye contact, giving short replies, stopping asking questions, glancing at their phone or watch, or becoming distracted. Any of these is a gentle cue that the topic has run its course and it is time to move on.

The three-step method. The smoothest way to change topic is to bridge from the old subject to the new one, rather than jumping. Think of it as: (old topic → new topic) + a question that links the two.

   1. Acknowledge the current subject, so the switch does not feel abrupt.
   2. Introduce the new topic with a clear connection to the old one.
   3. Invite the other person in with a question that bridges both.

An example. If someone is talking about aquariums and that is not your thing:

"I don’t really know a lot about aquariums, but I love aquatic animals — especially jellyfish. I wonder, can you actually keep a jellyfish in an aquarium?"

That one sentence gently closes the old topic, opens a new one you prefer, and hands the conversation back with a question — all without dismissing what they were saying.

Handy bridging phrases. Keep a few of these ready; they make a topic change feel natural:

   • "That reminds me of…"
   • "Speaking of which…"
   • "What you said reminded me of…"
   • "This may be a little off-topic, but…"

A few things that help. Keep your own stories fairly brief at first (around 30–60 seconds) so there is room for the other person. If they change the subject, try to go with the flow rather than steering back to your interest — following their lead is itself a social skill. And if you find topic changes stressful, it is perfectly fine to keep a short list of go-to topics or questions on your phone, so you are not relying on memory in the moment.
